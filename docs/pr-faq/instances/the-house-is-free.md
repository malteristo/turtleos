# The house is free

**Audience:** a person who can run turtleOS on a computer they own.
**Status:** Sanctioned as the offer, 2026-10-22 15:30. Not on the download page. The README stays as it is until a later word.

## Headline

turtleOS is a practice you run on context you keep. The software is free.

## Problem

A hosted chat is useful because it holds your context, and that context lives in the lab's account. The lab's product is the model plus the memory of you. A local tool is often free and leaves you to invent how the model should work. The valuable layer is not the model. It is the context, and the practice that uses it. A context kept in one company's account is also a single prize. Personal context belongs with the person, and it should be easy to use and easy to share on purpose. A page that pretends to sell the house, and later admits the sale was a study, would answer the wrong question and break the trust the project is for.

## Solution

turtleOS runs on a computer you own. You bring your own context. A practice is one way of working you return to, with a purpose that outlives a chat, on notes that stay on your machine. One practice at a time. You make it, or you take one from someone you trust and run it on your context. The agent in the room talks with you. A session is where a practice is built, carefully. Neither is a person.

The software is free either way. The page asks what you would have paid for this layer. Choosing a number adds it to a tally with no name on it, and does not take money. When enough people have actually paid, the page shows the middle of those amounts as the suggestion. Paying is support for a thing you already have. You can have the money back, with no questions. The page shows one way to write to the person who builds it. It does not ask for an email, and it does not run a tracker.

The kinds of practice:

- A room that is only yours, for thinking.
- One job with its own memory. A household, a portfolio, a piece of work.
- A room with the people you choose.
- A careful session in which you make the next practice.
- A practice someone you trust already made, running on your machine, on your context.

The value starts small. The first practices are the ones the person who builds the house runs for themselves. It grows when other people can make a practice and share how they made it. No shop is required for that.

## Benefits

- Your context stays with you, and the model can still be useful.
- You can trust a practice because you can see what it is working from.
- One practice at a time, made by you or taken from someone you trust.
- The house is the same whether you pay or not.
- The number you choose joins the suggestion other people see.
- Nothing on the page asks for an email address.
- Support comes back if you want it back.

## Practitioner quote

> It is all free. Keep your context.

## Getting started

Download turtleOS and run it on your machine. Today a new install creates one private room. You open an eddy and talk. That room is the first practice: thinking, on notes that stay there. The steps are the install on the README. The page does not take an address. Paying, if you do, is a bank transfer in euros for the amount you choose. There is no account to create.

## FAQ

### UX

A person should be able to say what a practice is, and that their context stays on their machine, before they are asked for a number. They should also be able to tell that the software is free and that choosing a price does not charge them. A payment is a separate act. The suggested amount, once it exists, is the middle of what people have actually paid, not a number the builder picked. Until there are ten real payments, the page shows no suggestion. A refund does not start a conversation.

The home room is where a person learns to make the next practice, including how a careful session differs from talking in the room. That guidance is the destination. A new install today does not yet do it.

### Not in scope

A price that unlocks a room. An account, an email field, or a tracker. A debrief that a payment was an experiment. Cryptocurrency. A shop, and a cut taken when someone shares a practice. Community created automatically at install. That last one remains the house in [practice-channels.md](../../design/practice-channels.md). This page does not claim it is true today. Replacing every other piece of technology is not the offer. One practice is.

### Approach

The model stays someone else's. The context, and the practice that uses it, stay with the person. The question and the payment are different acts, and the page says so. The question stores an amount and not a person. A payment is support, refundable, and the bank line exists because income has to be kept. That line is not a mailing list. The suggestion updates from real payments for as long as the page is up. The builder does not sit in the replies.

### Risks

People name higher amounts when no money moves. The tally is kept anyway, and it is not the suggestion. The suggestion waits for real payments, so a few generous answers cannot set it. A page that hid the free download until after a fake purchase would measure a locked product and would be a scam. This page shows the free house first. If the install is still hard, or a stranger cannot yet finish one practice, the amounts people name are about the promise. Those amounts stay out of the suggestion. A page that says the house replaces anything you do with technology would be asking for a concept nobody can try. A page that prices a platform of practices nobody else can make yet is pricing the promise. The suggestion waits until a person has used a house, and the first houses are the builder's own.

### Success / UX verification

A stranger can say back that a practice is one way of working on context they keep, that the software is free, that choosing a number does not charge them, and that paying is optional and refundable. They can name one kind of practice they could start with: a room of their own. After ten real payments, the suggested amount on the page is the middle of those payments. No email has been asked for. The README does not describe this offer until this page is sanctioned.
